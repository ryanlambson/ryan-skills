# KNOWLEDGE — Small Bar Drafting Standards (WA)

**Type:** HTS knowledge module (supporting evidence / drafting convention layer — never authority)
**Status:** Living module
**Version:** 1.0
**Created:** 5 June 2026
**Source of rules:** R.Lambson review amendments to the Rockshore Bistro Small Bar Change of Use DA (June 2026), verified against primary sources where regulatory.
**Consumed by:** lla-pia-drafter, lla-harm-minimisation, lla-gpt, lla-consumer-survey-drafter, lla-document-builder

This module encodes HTS house standards for drafting Small Bar applications and any
document describing a small bar manner of trade. It is a drafting-convention and
supporting-evidence layer. It is NOT an authority — legislation, regulations,
Director's Policy and case law continue to sit above it in the authority hierarchy.
Where a rule below states a regulatory fact, the primary source is cited and must be
re-verified at lodgement per the pre-delivery gate.

---

## 1. Regulatory facts (verify at lodgement)

- **Small bar maximum capacity = 150 persons (patrons PLUS staff) at any one time.**
  Source: *Liquor Control Act 1988* (WA), ss.41A/41B, as amended by the 2025 amendment
  package (increase from 120 to 150). Never state "120". Never frame 150 as patrons-only —
  it is patrons plus staff. Existing small bars may apply via Form 11 (fee waived) to lift
  120→150, subject to a safe-capacity assessment. Cross-referenced in the lla-citation-checker
  stale-citations registry (WARN on any "120" in a small bar capacity context).
- A small bar authorises sale of liquor **without a requirement that it be ancillary to a
  meal** — this is the defining distinction from a Restaurant licence and drives the
  terminology and manner-of-trade rules below.

## 2. Terminology

- Use **"patrons"**, not "persons", when describing capacity (e.g. "100 patrons").
- **Drop "dining"** in small bar contexts. Write **"alfresco area"** (not "alfresco dining
  area") and **"patron areas"** (not "dining areas"). "Dining" implies meal-tied service and
  mislabels a small bar.
- Council pathway: write **"DA or Section 40 Certificate"** — a change of use to a small bar
  may be processed by either route; do not presume one.

## 3. Manner of trade — state the small-bar-defining features explicitly

A small bar submission must positively describe what makes the use a small bar (not a
restaurant). Always include, where true:

- Patrons may **stand and mingle** and **need not be seated at all times** (the key
  distinction from a restaurant). Seating is provided throughout, but not mandatory.
- Patrons may **purchase liquor from the bar and carry it to a table or elsewhere** within
  the licensed area (i.e. service is not table-service-only).
- Capacity for **private cocktail bookings** where patrons socialise.
- Entertainment is **background in nature** — typically a **solo or duo acoustic artist**,
  not amplified or headline entertainment.

## 4. Right-size the impact sections — do not claim mitigation for impacts that do not arise

Overstating controls implies a problem exists. Match the content to the actual operation:

- For a venue running **background music only**, do not recite heavy noise-engineering
  (e.g. mechanical ventilation specifically for entertainment, "comprehensive noise
  measures"). Keep noise content proportionate, and do not list "noise management" among
  precinct emphases where it overstates the issue.
- Where operations are unchanged, **soften servicing content** (waste, noise) to describe
  continuity rather than asserting new facilities or controls that are not evidenced.

## 5. Anchor every "no change" argument to the causal hook

State the causation, do not merely assert continuity:

- "**No increase in patron numbers → no change to the waste / noise management plans.**"
- "As the proposal involves no change to the patron number, there is no change to the
  premises' waste servicing requirements."

This is the central planning argument for a format-only change of use and must be explicit.

## 6. Food & beverage description

Use **concrete, real detail** rather than generic "quality meals" — e.g. "contemporary,
modern Australian food, including share plates and lighter, healthier options". Specific
food detail supports the harm minimisation and amenity case, and reads as a genuine
hospitality offer rather than a drinking-focused venue.

## 7. Normalise the use

Note that small bars are an **established, contemporary format adopted across many
entertainment and dining precincts across WA** — this reduces perceived novelty/risk and
supports the public-interest case.

## 8. Document mechanics

- **Figure captions:** use Word SEQ caption fields (auto-renumbering), not hard-coded
  "Figure 1:" numbers. Check for duplicate sequence numbers before lodgement.
- **Attachment list must match what is actually lodged** — never carry template attachments
  (e.g. Certificates of Title, Sample Menu) that are not part of the bundle.
