# Authority hierarchy — regulatory-fact-refresh

When sources disagree about the current value of a regulatory fact, resolve in
this order (highest to lowest). A higher tier always prevails over a lower tier.
Within a single tier, the more recent source prevails.

1. Legislation as gazetted — the current consolidated text of an Act on the
   official legislation site (legislation.wa.gov.au for WA; the Federal Register
   of Legislation for Commonwealth). The Act as in force overrides everything
   below it.
2. Regulations — e.g. Liquor Control Regulations 1989, Planning and Development
   (Local Planning Schemes) Regulations 2015, as gazetted.
3. Operative planning schemes — Local Planning Scheme text as gazetted (e.g. LPS
   No. 1 and its operative amendments). Scheme text only, not draft.
4. Director's policies and regulator guidelines — DLGSC, LGIRS, WAPC,
   Department of Health, etc. The current published version on the regulator's
   own website. These can lag legislation; treat as guidance, not as the source
   of a statutory value.
5. Court and tribunal decisions — Supreme Court, Liquor Commission, SAT — from
   official decisions repositories (eCourts WA, AustLII, JADE).
6. Strategic planning instruments — Local Planning Strategies, Strategic
   Community Plans — current published version on the council's website.
7. Industry or professional-body publications — Retail Drinks Australia, NHMRC,
   ABS, etc. — current published edition.
8. Internal documents — used as sources to be verified, never as authorities. No
   circular verification: an internal document is never the basis for marking a
   claim CURRENT.

## Conflict rules

- Cross-tier conflict: the higher tier prevails. A regulator guideline (tier 4)
  that contradicts the gazetted Act (tier 1) does not override it; flag the
  guideline as lagging.
- Same-tier conflict: the more recent source prevails.
- A regulator guideline and the Act disagreeing is itself a signal — often a
  CHANGED-CONDITIONAL case where the law has moved and the guidance has not yet
  caught up, or vice versa. (Observed in practice: two DLGSC local-government
  guides simultaneously stating 120 and 150 for the small bar cap.)
