# LLA Writing Conventions

House writing rules for all Hospitality Total Services (HTS) liquor licensing documents — Public Interest Assessments, submissions, and supporting reports. Apply these rules at two points: while drafting new text, and as a review pass over any draft (including text not written in this session) before delivery.

## How to apply

When generating new licensing text, write to these conventions from the start. When reviewing or editing an existing draft, scan the full document for every rule below and correct each instance. After editing, run the verification checklist at the end and report what was changed.

These conventions are mandatory and override default phrasing. They do not override factual accuracy, citation discipline, or the PIA template structure — apply them within those constraints.

## Banned terms and required substitutions

Replace each banned term with its required substitute. Match all grammatical forms and capitalisations (e.g. plural, possessive, sentence-initial). Choose the substitute that reads naturally in context and preserve correct grammar after the swap.

| Banned term | Required substitute |
| --- | --- |
| sits (and "sit", "sitting" when describing location) | located or situated |
| food-led (and "food led") | hospitality |
| need (noun or verb) | requirement |
| community | consumer |

Notes on judgement:

- "sits" — use this rule only where the word describes where something is positioned (e.g. "the premises sits within the town centre" becomes "the premises is located within the town centre" or "is situated within the town centre"). Pick "located" or "situated" by readability. Do not alter unrelated idioms.
- "need" — recast the sentence so "requirement" fits grammatically. "The applicant needs to demonstrate" becomes "The applicant is required to demonstrate"; "there is a need for" becomes "there is a requirement for".
- "community" — substitute with "consumer" throughout. Where the original plural "communities" appears, use "consumers". Preserve the meaning of the sentence after substitution.
- Apply substitutions inside quotations from HTS-authored text, but never alter the wording of verbatim legislation, Director's Policy quotes, case law extracts, or third-party quoted material. If a banned term appears inside such a protected quote, leave the quote intact.

## CCTV wording

Whenever the document describes CCTV coverage, state that CCTV is located at the entry and exit points of the premises only. Do not describe CCTV as covering the whole premises, all internal areas, or the full floor area. The standard phrasing is:

> CCTV is located at the entry and exit points of the premises.

Adjust tense and surrounding wording to fit the sentence, but keep the entry-and-exit-points-only scope accurate.

## Em-dashes

Do not use em-dashes (—) inside paragraph text. Rewrite any em-dash construction using one of:

- a comma, where the dash sets off a brief aside
- a colon, where the dash introduces an explanation or list
- a full stop creating two sentences, where the dash joins two independent clauses
- parentheses, where the dash encloses a true aside

Also remove "spaced en-dash" substitutes ( – ) used as sentence punctuation in the same way. Hyphens in compound words (e.g. "town-centre", "well-being") are unaffected.

## Verification checklist

Before delivering any licensing document, confirm:

- [ ] No instance of "sits" / "sit" / "sitting" used to describe location remains; each is now "located" or "situated".
- [ ] No "food-led" / "food led" remains; replaced with "hospitality".
- [ ] No "need" / "needs" / "needing" remains as a general term; replaced with "requirement" forms and grammar corrected.
- [ ] No "community" / "communities" remains; replaced with "consumer" / "consumers".
- [ ] CCTV is described as located at the entry and exit points of the premises only.
- [ ] No em-dash (—) appears inside any paragraph; all rewritten with comma, colon, full stop, or parentheses.
- [ ] No banned term was substituted inside protected verbatim quotes (legislation, Director's Policy, case law, third-party quotes).
